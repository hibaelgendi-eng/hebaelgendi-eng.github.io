01 — Mobile gaming app · UX/UI case study

From discovery to play —
without losing clarity.

Mini Games gives players quick access to short, competitive games. Play. Compete. Win — held together by coins, rewards and leaderboards.

02 — The problem

A casual player has seconds of patience, not minutes.

Mini-game apps get cluttered fast: many games, categories, coins, rewards, ranks and progress all competing for the same screen. The design problem was to keep the path from opening the app to playing almost frictionless, while still carrying a competitive system underneath it.

03 — What I noticed

Four places where the experience could get in its own way.

01

Onboarding has to sell three ideas fast

Discover, rewards, compete — one per screen, each with a single Next and a visible Skip.

02

Browsing needs filters, not a wall

Search plus All / Arcade / Puzzle / Action, then a two-column grid of game art.

03

The result screen is the real loop

Score, rank, coins and time, then Play Again or Next Game — the session never dead-ends.

04

Competition needs a personal anchor

The leaderboard highlights "You" inside the list, not just the top three.

These are design observations from the interface, not validated research findings.

04 — Design opportunity

Make choosing a game feel like one tap — and make every result point at the next one.

Fast in

Skippable onboarding, social sign-in, search and category chips on the games screen.

Clear out

Score, rank, coins and time in one glance, with two obvious ways to continue.

A reason to return

Coins land in the wallet, rewards unlock, and the leaderboard shows where you stand.

05 — UX architecture

Four areas. One loop.

Home, Games, Ranks and Profile — discovery, playing and tracking results, rankings, and the player's own progress and rewards.

Open app Onboarding Create account / log in Browse & category Play Result Earn coins Play again / next game

06 — Entry & discovery

Three promises, then straight to the games.

Discover — what the app is, in one line.

Rewards — why playing keeps paying off.

Compete — the leaderboard as the long game.

Games — search, four categories, and art-led cards.

What I prioritised

Each onboarding card carries one heading, one sentence, a progress indicator and a single primary action — and SKIP stays available the whole time. Sign-up offers Google and Apple before the form, so account creation never blocks a first game. On the games screen, the coin balance sits in the header: the reward system is visible before the player earns anything.

07 — Result, rewards & ranks

The end of a game is where the system speaks.

Score first, then the three numbers that give it meaning — rank, coins earned, time — then the coins landing in the wallet, then two ways forward. Play Again keeps the same game; NEXT GAME, in the accent orange, pushes the session on.

Result — NEW SCORE badge, three stars, and the loop's two exits.

Reward unlocked — the coin amount is repeated as its own claimable object.

Ranks — podium for the top three, and "You" highlighted in place.

Failure state — same card language, one recovery action.

08 — Key design decisions

Five decisions the screens carry.

01

One action per screen

Every onboarding and auth screen has a single filled primary button; everything else is quieter.

02

Orange means "keep going"

Purple-blue carries the interface; orange is reserved for progression and rewards.

03

Coins are always in frame

The balance sits in the header on games and ranks, so earning connects to a running total.

04

Four tabs, no more

Home, Games, Ranks, Profile — persistent orientation, even on the result screen.

05

Cards for every message

Onboarding, rewards and errors reuse the same rounded panel — new states cost nothing to add.

09 — Visual system

Dark neon, kept readable.

Deep navy surfaces, purple-blue gradients, glowing focal circles, rounded cards, and Nunito set bold for hierarchy — energetic without making the interface hard to read.
